Where you actually are

You're two days into a restart after a 44-day total gap (last session before this streak: 18 Jun). Translation: your base fitness hasn't evaporated, but the body is registering these two rucks as a fresh stimulus, not routine load yet. That's exactly what a 6-week layoff looks like — no red flags, just early days.

Readiness after wake-up was 80/HIGH, blended score for the day 69/MODERATE. Nothing here says stop — the one soft flag is sleep duration, not recovery capacity.

The plan for the rest of August

Week 1 (3–7 Aug): keep doing what Aug 1–2 already did — 3–5 km, easy pace, HR mostly Zone 1–2. The job this week isn't distance, it's not missing a day. If a day's sleep score comes in under ~50 or HRV drops out of the balanced band, that's your cue for the shortest version (20–30 min, flat, no plate) rather than skipping — the streak survives, the joints don't get punished.
Week 2 (8–14 Aug): if HR is trending down at the same pace/distance and sleep has evened out, start stretching the longer days toward 6–8 km. Keep at least one short day (3 km) in the mix — daily doesn't have to mean equal, it means never zero.
Week 3–4 (15–31 Aug): build toward one longer ruck a week (8–10 km, the range your best May/Oct sessions sat in) with the plate at full load, bracketed by shorter recovery days either side. This mirrors the pattern that actually worked in October–May: long days never landed back to back.
Watch for: the ACWR ratio is the one number worth tracking weekly — it's what flagged nothing wrong so far because the base is low, but it'll start moving fast as chronic load catches up to the new daily habit. If it jumps into "HIGH" territory in the readiness data, that's the actual signal to back off a day, not a feeling.
