The Tic Tac

NIMITZ ENCOUNTER (FRAVOR / DIETRICH) · 14 NOV 2004 · PACIFIC OCEAN, ~100MI SW SAN DIEGO
VERDICT: UNRESOLVED

Four Navy pilots visually observed an object with no wings, no exhaust, no propulsion. All agree it departed at a speed beyond anything they could follow. Mundane hypotheses cannot account for the irreducible core testimony.

The method: falsification, not confirmation. For each hypothesis, ask one question — can the available evidence rule it out? If yes, it's dead. If no, it survives — whether mundane or extraordinary. Surviving hypotheses are not ranked against each other; no priors, no probabilities, no ideology. Three tiers of falsification, strongest to weakest: decisive (instrument data / physics contradicts it outright), testimonial (incompatible with consistent multi-witness testimony, conditional on that testimony being accurate), plausibility (not impossible, just strained past the point of a good explanation).

What Was Observed

Two F/A-18F Super Hornets (four crew total) were diverted from a training exercise by the USS Princeton to investigate a radar contact. In clear daylight over calm seas, all four crew observed a disturbance in the water below, and a white, oblong, wingless, propulsion-less object roughly 30–40 feet long above it. Commander David Fravor descended to engage. The object departed at extreme speed, vanishing from close visual range in under a second. No exhaust trail. No sonic boom.

A second flight with an ATFLIR pod launched about an hour later. Lt. Cmdr. Chad Underwood recorded the FLIR1 infrared video — it shows the oblong object, but not the extraordinary departure the first crew described. Fravor's own jet didn't carry an ATFLIR pod, so the most extraordinary moment of the whole encounter was never on camera.

Irreducible Core — What All Four Agree On

  • Diverted by Princeton to investigate a real radar contact
  • Calm, clear day, excellent visibility
  • A disturbance in the water on an otherwise calm sea
  • White, oblong object, ~30–40ft, no wings, no markings, no exhaust
  • Fravor descended; Dietrich stayed high as overwatch
  • Object departed at extreme speed — "shot out of a rifle"
  • Written debriefs completed within hours of landing

Where Witnesses Diverge

Duration: Fravor says ~5 minutes of visual contact; Dietrich says 8–10 seconds. Unclarified.
Whitewater shape: Fravor describes a cross-shape; Dietrich a cylindrical shape.
Behaviour before departure: Fravor describes hovering and interactive mirroring of his descent; Dietrich's language is more ambiguous, elsewhere describing straight-line motion at 300–500 knots.
Speed before departure: Fravor's own WSO reported the object "traveling level at 500 knots" in the executive summary — inconsistent with Fravor's account of near-stationary hovering.

Hypotheses Tested

HypothesisStatusReasoning
Balloon Ruled out Testimonial. Accounts for hovering and lack of propulsion, not the departure. A balloon cannot go from near-stationary to invisible-at-half-a-mile in under a second. All four witnesses agree on the extreme-speed departure.
Cruise missile Ruled out Testimonial. Accounts for rapid departure and shape, not the hovering above whitewater. Missiles leave visible exhaust; none was seen. Dietrich — who has seen thousands of missiles — was shown missile footage and immediately distinguished it from what she saw. No launch was logged.
Conventional drone (2004-era) Ruled out Testimonial. No 2004-era drone matches the size, shape, flight characteristics, or departure speed described. No wings or rotors observed at close range in daylight.
Advanced classified US craft Survives Can't be ruled out in principle — but requires a programme producing technology far beyond publicly known physics, keeping perfect secrecy for 20+ years, and being tested without coordination near an active carrier strike group. Plausible, but with its own extraordinary assumptions.
Sensor malfunction / misperception Ruled out Testimonial. Four crew in two aircraft made visual contact in daylight — not a single-sensor event.
Fabrication Ruled out Plausibility. Four career officers — including Dietrich, who actively resists the extraordinary framing — held consistent core testimony for 20 years, including under oath before Congress. Dietrich waited 17 years to come forward and calls the experience absurd. Not the profile of coordinated fabrication.
Anomalous / unknown physics Survives The irreducible core — a physical object, seen by four people in daylight, going from near-stationary to beyond visual range in under a second with no propulsion or sonic boom — isn't explained by any known technology or natural phenomenon.

Evidence Tiers

Strongest (direct visual, multi-witness): object appearance, whitewater, departure speed — four witnesses, daylight, clear conditions.
Weaker (single-witness testimony about instruments): the claim the object reappeared on radar 60 miles away seconds after departing rests mainly on radar operator Kevin Day's account. Day has shown detail drift elsewhere (a "barrel roll" Fravor denies). The actual radar data no longer exists. Fravor only heard about the reappearance by radio.
Weakest (disputed between witnesses): duration, the "mirroring" behaviour, the exact whitewater shape — differ between Fravor and Dietrich, can't be independently verified.

What Would Change This

To resolve mundane: public testimony from the two WSOs contradicting the departure account; identification of a specific test programme in the area that day; evidence all four accounts trace back to one contaminated source.
To resolve as confirmed anomalous: the original Princeton radar data; ATFLIR or other footage of the departure itself; physical trace evidence; independent corroboration of the radar reappearance from a second named source.

Current Status

The irreducible core eyewitness testimony survives every mundane hypothesis tested. The strongest corroborating evidence — the original radar data — no longer exists. The case rests on human testimony, which is the strongest evidence available for an event like this, but it is not instrument data. Two hypotheses remain standing: an unacknowledged classified craft, or something genuinely unexplained.
